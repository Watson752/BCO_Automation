# Developer Tools, Infrastructure, and API Sponsor Candidates

## Which companies are strongly qualified for the EasyLens x Snapchat Toronto workshop/hackathon?

### Takeaway
Seven companies clear the mandatory email gate and have concrete Canadian relevance or a Canada-accessible developer program. MongoDB, DigitalOcean, Datadog, Sentry, and Supabase are the strongest first wave; GitLab and Cloudflare are qualified but should receive tightly scoped, low-friction asks because the November 12 event is only 17 days away.

### Inferences

- **Recommended outreach order:** Supabase first because it publishes a dedicated event-sponsorship inbox; then Datadog and MongoDB because of current Toronto event operations; then DigitalOcean and Sentry because their Toronto infrastructure/team presence and credit programs make in-kind support plausible; then GitLab and Cloudflare as narrowly scoped backup asks.
- **Ask design:** Do not ask each company for the full CAD $3,000-$4,000 event budget. A realistic portfolio is one CAD $750-$1,000 anchor, two CAD $500-$750 contributors, and the remainder in credits, prizes, food, swag, or mentors.
- **Timing:** With 17 days remaining, lead every note with a binary, easy-to-approve package and a reply deadline. Credits, swag, speakers, and existing student programs are more plausible than net-new procurement.
- **Email quality:** Supabase and Datadog provide role-aligned official inboxes. GitLab provides a community/Developer Relations inbox. MongoDB, DigitalOcean, Sentry, and Cloudflare clear the exact-email gate, but their published addresses are routing contacts; outreach should explicitly ask for forwarding and should not imply those teams own sponsorship.
- **Personalization:** The highest-conversion hooks are MongoDB’s November 19 Toronto event, DigitalOcean’s TOR1/October agent launches, Datadog’s 2026 Toronto AI-observability programming, Sentry’s Toronto entity and prior Toronto agent-build event, and Supabase’s explicit sponsorship channel plus Canada region.

### Gaps

- No source confirms that any company will approve this specific event, amount, or contribution; all recommended contributions are calibrated asks based on documented programs and local activity.
- MongoDB, DigitalOcean, Sentry, and Cloudflare did not expose a first-party sponsorship-specific email in the reviewed official pages. Their retained addresses are exact, official, and company-owned, but routing risk is higher.
- Cloudflare’s published student developer benefit is U.S.-only. Any Canadian credits must be separately approved; the outreach must not promise this program to attendees.
- GitLab’s normal lead-time guidance is incompatible with a November 12 event, reducing the probability of cash support.
- Sentry’s most relevant Toronto hackathon evidence is on Vercel’s official community domain rather than sentry.io; Sentry’s own official pages independently establish its Toronto entity, Toronto hiring, and student sponsorship capacity.
- Exact Canadian dollar budgets, local decision-makers, inventory availability, and response SLAs are not publicly documented.
